Three directions with an actual point of view

Round one drew on software convention. These three draw on the subject's own world — the telephone exchange, the timing diagram, and the message thread — so the identity comes from what the product does rather than from what other B2B products look like.

Specimens render in system fonts because published pages can't load font CDNs. Production typefaces are named in each spec panel; the weights, spacing, and hierarchy shown are the real proposal.

Direction 1

Switchboard

A bridge is a telephony term — it's what connects one line to another. This direction takes the name literally and builds the identity out of the telephone exchange: patch cables, jack fields, and lamps that tell an operator the state of every line.

Palette

1A1410 bakelite 241C16 panel C9A227 brass 3FA787 connected C4462F dropped F0E7DA bone

Warm brown-black rather than navy, so brass reads as metal and the lamp colors read as status hardware. Jade and red are literal signal lamps, which means semantic color is built into the metaphor instead of bolted on.

Type

Cabinet Grotesk for display, set uppercase and tight — industrial without going condensed-poster. Switzer for body and app UI. Space Mono for line labels and timestamps; its slightly mechanical shapes belong to this world in a way a neutral mono doesn't.

Signature & risk

The patch cable — a single brass curve crossing from a ringing line to a booked slot. It is the company name drawn as a picture, and it scales from a logo mark to a full hero animation.

The risk: heritage can slide into costume. Kept to one cable and a lamp field it reads confident; add texture, grain, or skeuomorphic metal and it becomes a theme restaurant.

Direction 2

Timing

The product is a sequence, and the thing you sell is the gap between two of its steps. This direction makes the timing diagram the whole identity: an ultramarine ground, one ochre tick per step, and the elapsed time set enormous.

Seven steps. One of them is why you lose patients.

Step two is the whole business. Answer inside five minutes and the patient stops shopping; answer in four hours and you are talking to someone who already booked elsewhere. We own every step and report the timing on all of them.

Palette

1B2C87 ultramarine 22349B panel E0A526 tick F0EFE8 bone A9B3DD muted

A saturated ultramarine ground instead of the usual white or near-black. It is the one decision that keeps this from looking like every analytics product, and ochre is the only hue that holds its own against it.

Type

Switzer for display and UI — Swiss-neutral, which is correct here because the diagram carries the personality and the type should not compete. JetBrains Mono at display sizes for elapsed times. Numerals are the largest thing on the page.

Signature & risk

The rail: seven ticks, numbered, with real elapsed times between them. Numbering is earned here — the content genuinely is an ordered sequence and the gaps carry the argument.

The risk: a saturated ground is expensive to live with. Every screenshot, PDF report, and email template has to work on ultramarine or deliberately break to bone, and that decision has to be made once and held.

Direction 3

Thread

Everything the patient experiences happens in a text message. This direction puts the actual conversation on the page at display size — the product's voice becomes the product's typography.

Palette

F7F3F1 ground 7A2E42 wine E8926A apricot 241726 ink E3D8DC rule

Wine for the practice's own voice, apricot reserved for elapsed time — the one number that matters. Deliberately not iMessage blue or WhatsApp green, so the thread reads as this brand's rather than a screenshot of someone else's app.

Type

Sentient for display — a warm literary serif, because the argument is that automation can still sound human. Switzer for UI and app density. Space Mono for timestamps. Message copy sets at near-body size and is the most important type on the page.

Signature & risk

The thread itself, shown at full size with real copy and a highlighted response time. It is the product demo, the proof, and the hero image at once, and it costs nothing to keep fresh — every client generates new ones.

The risk: the marketing site nearly designs itself, but the app does not. Dense operational screens get little help from this direction, so the agency dashboard needs a deliberate second act built on the same tokens.

Where I'd land

Direction 2, Timing. It is the only one of the three whose signature device is also a genuine product surface: that rail is the hero on the marketing site, the timeline on a lead's detail screen in the app, and the top of every monthly report. Switchboard has the better story and the better logo, but the metaphor is decorative once you are inside the app — nothing in a dashboard is actually a patch cable. Thread has the strongest marketing page of the three and the weakest app.

If the ultramarine ground feels like too much to commit to, the honest hedge is to hold ultramarine for the marketing site and brand moments, and run the app on bone with ultramarine as its primary — one token set, two grounds.
