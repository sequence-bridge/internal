§ 01 The Company

The Founders are building an AI, product, and software consultancy (the "Company," currently referred to as Riamatic) that sells productized AI services to small businesses, with an initial focus on the investing sector (small RIAs). The Founders intend to form the Company as a Florida limited liability company (LLC). Until the LLC is formed and an Operating Agreement is executed, this Term Sheet governs the Founders' relationship.

§ 02 Ownership / Equity Split

  • The Founders will own the Company 50% / 50% as equal members, subject to the vesting terms in §8.
  • Each Founder holds a 50% membership interest, carrying equal voting rights except where §7 provides otherwise.

§ 03 Capital Contributions ("Investment")

  • Capital is contributed in proportion to equity — i.e., 50/50. Whatever cash the Company needs, each Founder funds half.
  • No fixed initial contribution is committed at this stage. Contributions are made as real costs arise (tools, software, formation/legal fees, vendors, marketing), by mutual agreement, and split 50/50.
  • Shortfall mechanism (recommended default). If one Founder funds more than their 50% share of an agreed expense, the excess is treated as a member loan to the Company, repaid without interest before profit distributions — so the 50/50 equity split is preserved rather than diluted. Any grace period for evening up a shortfall is agreed by the Founders at the time, case by case.
  • Non-cash contributions (time, labor, existing relationships, IP) are recognized through the roles, vesting, and IP terms below — they do not change the 50/50 cash-contribution principle.

§ 06 Compensation & Profit Distribution

  • The Founders take no salary during the Building Phase.
  • Once revenue supports it, salaries or owner draws may be introduced by mutual agreement, sized to what the Company can sustain.
  • After expenses, reserves, and any member-loan repayments (§3), profits and losses are allocated and distributed 50/50, in line with the equity split.
  • The Company will aim to make tax distributions sufficient to cover each Founder's allocated taxable income (subject to the cross-border considerations in §18).

§ 07 Decision Rights & Governance

  • Within their domain (§5), each Founder decides independently.
  • Shared decisions are made by consensus — the Founders talk it through and aim to agree.
  • Tie-breaker: If the Founders genuinely cannot agree on a shared decision, Hector has the final say.
Fundamental Matters — mutual consent (recommended carve-out)

Because Hector's tie-breaker otherwise gives one Founder broad control of an equally owned company, the following require both Founders' written consent and are not subject to the tie-breaker:

  1. Dissolving, selling, or merging the Company, or selling substantially all its assets;
  2. Admitting a new owner, or issuing/transferring equity;
  3. Changing the equity split or either Founder's economic rights;
  4. Incurring debt, loans, or guarantees above [$amount];
  5. Taking distributions disproportionate to ownership;
  6. Amending this Term Sheet or the future Operating Agreement;
  7. Related-party transactions or anything materially benefiting one Founder over the other.

The Founders may keep, narrow, or remove this carve-out — but removing it means Hector's tie-breaker would extend even to the items above. Discuss before deciding.

§ 08 Vesting & Cliff

To protect both Founders if one leaves early, each Founder's 50% interest vests over four (4) years rather than being owned outright on day one:

  • Vesting period: 4 years, vesting in equal monthly increments (1/48 per month).
  • Cliff: 1 year. Nothing vests during the first 12 months; at the 12-month anniversary, 25% vests in a lump, then monthly thereafter.
  • Vesting start date: the date this Term Sheet is signed by both Founders.
  • Unvested interest is subject to repurchase by the Company (or the remaining Founder) under §9.
  • No acceleration by default. The Founders may later agree to acceleration on a sale of the Company [optional — discuss].

§ 09 Departure / Leaver Provisions

If a Founder leaves (voluntarily resigns, becomes unable to continue, or is removed for cause):

  • Unvested interest (per §8) is forfeited and may be repurchased by the Company or the remaining Founder at nominal value (the lesser of cost or par).
  • Vested interest:
    • Good leaver (e.g., departure by mutual agreement, incapacity, or other no-fault reason): the leaving Founder keeps their vested interest, subject to the transfer restrictions in §10 and a Company/remaining-Founder right to buy it out at fair value [valuation method TBD].
    • Bad leaver (e.g., fraud, gross misconduct, material uncured breach of this agreement, or competing with the Company in breach of §11): the Company or remaining Founder may repurchase even the vested interest at the lower of cost or fair value.
  • The departing Founder steps down from their role and returns Company property and access.

§ 10 Transfer Restrictions

  • Neither Founder may sell, pledge, or transfer their interest without the other's prior written consent.
  • Any permitted transfer is subject to a right of first refusal: the interest must first be offered to the Company and then to the remaining Founder on the same terms.

§ 11 Confidentiality, Non-Solicit & Non-Compete

  • Each Founder keeps the Company's confidential information (client data, methods, pricing, code, content) confidential during and after their involvement.
  • During involvement and for [12 months] after departure, neither Founder will solicit the Company's clients or team, nor operate a competing productized-AI-services business in the same niche.

Florida enforces reasonable restrictive covenants (Fla. Stat. § 542.335), but duration and scope must be reasonable — confirm with counsel (§17).

§ 12 Intellectual Property

  • All work product created for the Company — code, agent designs, content, methodologies, client deliverables, and the Company brand — is owned by the Company.
  • Each Founder assigns to the Company any pre-existing IP they bring in and use in the business, and waives claims to Company IP beyond their ownership interest.

§ 13 Dispute Resolution & Governing Law

  • The Founders will first attempt to resolve disputes through good-faith discussion, then mediation, before any formal proceeding.
  • This agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Florida; venue is [county TBD].

§ 14 Term & Binding Effect

  • This Term Sheet takes effect on signing and is a good-faith, binding agreement between the two Founders as to their intent.
  • The Founders will work in good faith to execute a formal Florida LLC Operating Agreement reflecting these terms (and standard legal provisions) within [60–90 days] of forming the LLC. The Operating Agreement, once signed, supersedes this Term Sheet.
  • If the Founders never form the entity or sign an Operating Agreement, this Term Sheet continues to govern their relationship.

§ 15 Amendment

This Term Sheet may be amended only in writing, signed by both Founders (see also the Fundamental Matters carve-out in §7).

§ 16 Entire Understanding

This document reflects the Founders' complete understanding on the matters covered and replaces any prior verbal arrangements between them.

§ 17 Legal Review Note

Before you form the entity

This is a plain-language founders' agreement, not a lawyer-drafted operating agreement. Before forming the LLC and finalizing terms, have a Florida-licensed business attorney review and convert this into a proper Operating Agreement — particularly the vesting/repurchase mechanics (§8–§9), restrictive covenants (§11), and IP assignment (§12).

§ 18 Cross-Border Note (both Founders in Ecuador)

US / Ecuador — all-foreign ownership

Both Founders are resident in Ecuador, so the planned Florida LLC would be a US entity owned entirely by non-US-resident members. This carries US tax and compliance obligations a US-only LLC does not — for example, potential US withholding on each member's share of US-effectively-connected income, additional federal filings (e.g., Forms 1042 / 8804 / 8805), ITINs for both Founders, and the Ecuadorian tax treatment of US-source income and distributions. Whether a Florida LLC is even the right vehicle — versus a C-corp or another structure — depends heavily on this all-foreign-ownership profile. Engage a US/Ecuador cross-border tax advisor before forming the entity so the structure is set up correctly from day one.

Informational flag only, not tax advice.
